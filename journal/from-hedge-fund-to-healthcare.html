Journey 2024 · Origins

From hedge fund to healthcare: how Chryzylis began

Blake started investing in 2017, building what would become a boutique hedge fund focused on long/short equity strategies. It was rigorous, analytical work — but it was also, fundamentally, about markets and capital rather than people and operations.

The shift came through building businesses, not just analysing them. Scaling a recruitment business from the ground up taught lessons no spreadsheet could: how culture compounds, how much retention matters, and how the people decisions a founder makes early on shape everything that follows.

Why community healthcare

Becoming an active operator in domiciliary care sharpened the focus further. Here was a sector defined by genuine necessity — businesses serving people at some of the most vulnerable moments in their lives — and yet structurally fragmented, owned by founders who had built deep, trusted relationships with almost no institutional support behind them.

That combination — essential service, fragmented ownership, founders nearing succession decisions without an obvious next step — is exactly where Chryzylis was built to operate. Domiciliary care, dental practices, opticians, and the wider allied health sector all share this pattern.

"I didn't want to build a fund that extracts value from healthcare. I wanted to build a firm that protects it." — Blake

Where we are now

Chryzylis operates today with that same operator's mindset at its core — supported by an advisory bench across finance, law and operations, and guided by a twenty-year investment horizon. Every acquisition since has reinforced the same conviction: the businesses that matter most to their communities deserve owners who will protect, not just profit from, what's been built.
