Reflection 2025 · From Blake

A letter to owners thinking about retirement

I've now had this conversation dozens of times, and it almost never starts with money. It starts with a long pause, and then something like: "I just want to know they'll be looked after."

That's the part most acquirers miss. By the time an owner is ready to talk seriously about selling, they've usually already made peace with the financial side. What keeps them up at night is everything else — the staff who've been with them for fifteen years, the patients or clients who trust the name above the door, the reputation built one decision at a time over a working lifetime.

The hardest part isn't the price

If you're approaching this stage, you already know the deal terms matter less than you'd think. What matters is who you're handing the keys to, and whether they'll treat your life's work as a legacy to protect or an asset to strip.

I've operated a domiciliary care business myself. I've made payroll on a tight month, dealt with a CQC inspection, and sat across the table from a member of staff worried about their job security during a change of ownership. That experience shapes every conversation I have with sellers — I'm not asking you to trust a stranger with a spreadsheet. I'm asking you to trust someone who has stood where you're standing.

"There's no pressure, no jargon, and no intermediaries. Just a conversation between two people who've built something." — Blake

What a good conversation looks like

We start by listening. What do you want for your team? What does a dignified retirement look like to you? What parts of the business are non-negotiable, and which are open to change? Only once we understand that do we talk numbers — because the structure of a deal should serve the legacy, not the other way around.
