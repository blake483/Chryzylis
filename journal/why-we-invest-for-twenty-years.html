Reflection 2025 · Investment Philosophy

Why we invest for twenty years, not five

Most private equity is built around a five-year hold. Buy, improve, sell. It's a model that works well for businesses designed to be traded — but it sits uneasily with the kind of businesses we acquire: domiciliary care providers, dental practices, opticians. Places where patients, families and staff measure trust in decades, not quarters.

When a founder sells their business into a five-year structure, they are, in effect, selling it twice — once to us, and once again to whoever buys it from us. Every layer of that process introduces risk to the things that matter most: continuity of care, staff retention, and the culture the founder spent a career building.

A different structure, deliberately

Chryzylis is built around a twenty-year investment horizon. There is no fund clock forcing an exit, no pressure to extract short-term value ahead of a sale process. That changes the incentives entirely — we are aligned with the long-term health of the business, not with dressing it up for the next buyer.

In practice, this means we invest in things a five-year owner might skip: staff training, technology that improves care quality, succession planning for management teams. These are investments that take years to pay off — exactly the kind of investment a founder would have made themselves, had they stayed.

"We're not building a portfolio to flip. We're building a institution that will still be here in twenty years." — Blake, Founder

What this means for sellers

If you're weighing up an offer from Chryzylis against a traditional PE buyer, the difference isn't just in price — it's in what happens after completion. We're not optimising for a future sale. We're optimising for the business, the people in it, and the legacy you're trusting us with.
